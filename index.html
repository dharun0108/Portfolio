Case study 01Healthcare interoperabilityApp Innovation Technologies

TeleMEDIx

A clinical data normalization engine that turns raw longitudinal medical records into standardized, fully-coded patient snapshots for clinical decision support.

Context

Clinical decision support only works on data it can trust. Longitudinal medical records arrive as heterogeneous documents from many sources, with the same medication, lab or problem written a dozen different ways.

Engineering challenge

Standardize records across roughly 25 clinical sections — problems, medications, labs, vitals, allergies and more — into one canonical shape, map every value to the right terminology, and compute 15 clinical risk scores in a way that is explainable, not a black box.

My contribution

  • Built the engine end to end in .NET on ASP.NET Minimal API.
  • Designed the ~25 section pipelines mapping raw clinical data to LOINC, SNOMED CT, ICD-10, RxNorm, CPT and FDA terminologies.
  • Implemented 15 risk scores — PREVENT, ASCVD-PCE, CHA₂DS₂-VASc, HAS-BLED, HEART and more — each returning the risk value, tier, inputs used and coefficient source.
  • Built a cache-first data flow: Azure Blob Storage with a Cosmos DB reference index, MongoDB for terminology lookups.
  • Added confidence scoring and full provenance on every normalized record.

Technologies

.NET · C# · ASP.NET Minimal API · MongoDB · Azure Cosmos DB · Azure Blob Storage · Redis

Outcome

Standardized, fully-coded patient snapshots ready for downstream clinical decision support — every value traceable to its source, every risk score explainable through its inputs and coefficient source.

Clinical sections ~25

Problems · Medications · Labs · Vitals · Allergies · Encounters · Procedures · Immunizations · and more

Risk scores 15

PREVENT · ASCVD-PCE · CHA₂DS₂-VASc · HAS-BLED · HEART · and 10 more — each returning risk value, tier, inputs used and coefficient source

Terminologies 6

LOINC · SNOMED CT · ICD-10 · RxNorm · CPT · FDA

Engineering notes

  • Cache-first: a Blob Storage layer fronted by a Cosmos DB reference index avoids re-processing records already normalized.
  • Terminology lookups are served from MongoDB so mapping stays fast as vocabularies grow.
  • Every record carries a confidence score and provenance, so clinicians and downstream systems can see how a coded value was derived.
Case study 02Telehealth platformApp Innovation Technologies

.NET 8 Telehealth Backend

The REST API behind a telehealth platform that consolidates a patient's scattered hospital records into one unified health record and runs the full care workflow with AI clinical support.

Context

A patient's history is spread across hospitals, networks and devices. The platform needed one record per patient — and everything around it: onboarding, consent, messaging, visits and payments.

Engineering challenge

Discover and retrieve records across health information networks, verify identity before anything is pulled, parse C-CDA and FHIR documents (~27 clinical sections) into one longitudinal record, and keep every exchange auditable under HIPAA-aligned handling.

My contribution

  • JWT authentication with role-based access and per-device refresh tokens; multi-step patient onboarding and HIPAA consent management.
  • Health-record aggregation via Kno2 (TEFCA / Carequality) and Epic FHIR with identity verification prior to retrieval.
  • Secure communication: patient–doctor in-app chat, plus document exchange over Kno2 fax and Direct Secure Messaging with delivery auditing and read-receipt notifications.
  • Telehealth video visits — booking, availability, per-visit and subscription payments — with live transcription and AI-generated SOAP notes.
  • Wearable vitals from Fitbit, Apple HealthKit, Garmin and Google Fit, processed as Hangfire background jobs.

Chapter 06The Impact

Why fragmented records are worth fixing.

A clinician rarely sees a patient's whole history. It lives in several hospitals, in different document formats, using different words for the same thing. The systems I build retrieve those records, translate them into a shared vocabulary and hand back one structured, longitudinal record that decision-support logic can actually reason about.

Records are discovered and retrieved from the hospitals that hold them.

Through Kno2 on the TEFCA and Carequality networks and directly from Epic via FHIR. Identity is verified before any retrieval, and every exchange — including Direct Secure Messaging and fax — is audited.

Documents are parsed into their clinical sections.

C-CDA and FHIR documents are broken down into roughly 27 sections — problems, medications, labs, vitals, allergies, encounters and more — so that content from different hospitals lines up side by side.

Every value is translated into a shared clinical vocabulary.

TeleMEDIx maps raw entries to LOINC, SNOMED CT, ICD-10, RxNorm and CPT, attaching a confidence score and provenance to each coded value. The same lab means the same thing regardless of where it was recorded.

One longitudinal record becomes the input to decision support.

The canonical patient snapshot feeds 15 clinical risk scores — PREVENT, ASCVD-PCE, CHA₂DS₂-VASc, HAS-BLED, HEART and others — each returning its value, tier, inputs and coefficient source so a clinician can see why.
